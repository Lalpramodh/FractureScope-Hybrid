Common Fracture Locations

Wrist Fracture

Wrist fractures, especially distal radius fractures, are among the most common. These injuries typically result from falls onto an outstretched hand. Symptoms include pain, swelling, bruising, and limited wrist motion. Early detection is key for proper alignment and healing.

Elbow Fracture

Elbow fractures may involve the radial head, olecranon, or distal humerus. They are usually caused by a fall or direct blow and are characterized by swelling, sharp pain, and difficulty in bending the arm. Treatment may vary from immobilization to surgical repair.

Humerus Fracture

Humerus fractures occur in the upper arm bone and can result from high-impact trauma or falls. These fractures are divided into proximal, mid-shaft, and distal types. Common symptoms include pain, swelling, deformity, and restricted arm movement.

Shoulder Fracture

Shoulder fractures often involve the clavicle (collarbone), scapula, or the upper part of the humerus. They are caused by trauma such as car accidents or sports injuries. Patients may experience shoulder instability, swelling, and intense pain.

Hand Fracture

Hand fractures can involve the bones of the fingers (phalanges) or the metacarpals. Common in contact sports or falls, symptoms include pain, swelling, bruising, and difficulty moving the hand. Prompt diagnosis ensures proper alignment and function recovery.
